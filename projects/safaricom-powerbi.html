01 · Business Problem

How do we turn 10 years of audited financials into CFO-ready strategy intelligence?

Safaricom is East Africa's largest telecommunications company and one of the most strategically important listed companies on the Nairobi Securities Exchange. Leadership needs a dashboard that moves beyond basic revenue charts — one that explains the economic story behind the numbers.

The challenge was building a single dashboard that could surface M-PESA revenue trends, identify anomalies in reported earnings, explain capex spikes, and present IFRS-adjusted metrics in a way that non-technical executives can act on.

The economic context: Safaricom's financials contain several analytically important events that a basic dashboard would miss: the Ethiopia IAS 29 hyperinflation gain anomaly that inflated 2023 reported earnings, the M-PESA COVID zero-rating inflection point in FY2020, and the FY2022 capex spike that was widely misread as infrastructure investment but was actually the Ethiopia market entry cost.
02 · Data Source

Ernst & Young Audited Financial Data

The primary data source for this project was Safaricom's Ernst & Young audited annual financial statements spanning 10 fiscal years. All figures are IFRS-adjusted, meaning the analysis uses the same accounting framework as institutional investors and CFOs — not publicly scraped or estimated data.

04 · Advanced DAX

Selected DAX measures built for this dashboard

YoY Revenue Growth %
Uses PARALLELPERIOD to compare current year revenue against prior year — same period, same currency basis
Revenue Color Hex
Conditional color measure — returns hex code "#22c55e" for positive YoY growth, "#ef4444" for decline, "#C9A84C" for flat. Applied to YoY labels dynamically.
EBITDA Margin %
EBITDA divided by total revenue — calculated from IFRS-adjusted line items, not estimated
MPESA Revenue Share
M-PESA revenue as a percentage of total group revenue — shows the platform's increasing dependence on M-PESA over the 10-year period
Capex Intensity
Capex divided by revenue — context measure that shows FY2022 spike was a strategic cost, not an infrastructure upgrade cycle
AVERAGEX Margin by Year
AVERAGEX over years table to smooth margin volatility caused by the Ethiopia hyperinflation gain anomaly in FY2023
05 · Key Analytical Findings

What the dashboard reveals that basic charts miss

Ethiopia IAS 29 Hyperinflation Gain Anomaly: Safaricom's FY2023 reported earnings included a significant hyperinflation accounting gain under IAS 29 from the Ethiopia operation. This inflated reported profitability materially. An analyst reading only the headline numbers would overestimate earnings quality. The dashboard flags this as a one-time accounting item, not operational performance.
M-PESA COVID Zero-Rating Inflection Point: In FY2020, Safaricom zero-rated M-PESA transactions in response to COVID-19. The short-term revenue impact was visible — but the long-term effect was a step-change increase in M-PESA user adoption and transaction frequency. The FY2021 recovery was not a bounce-back; it was a structurally higher baseline.
FY2022 Capex Spike — Strategic Acquisition, Not Infrastructure: The FY2022 capex figure is approximately 60% higher than the prior year average. This is consistently misread as a network infrastructure cycle. It was the capitalized cost of the Ethiopia market entry and license acquisition — a strategic bet, not routine capex.
06 · Recommendations

CFO-level insights from the dashboard

  • Strip out the Ethiopia IAS 29 hyperinflation gain from FY2023 earnings when assessing underlying business performance. Adjusted EBITDA is materially lower than reported EBITDA for that year.
  • Model M-PESA revenue separately from voice and data when forecasting. M-PESA has a structurally different growth trajectory — it is now the primary revenue driver and the business should be valued and managed accordingly.
  • Treat FY2022 capex as a one-time strategic cost in any capex/revenue ratio analysis. Including it in trend calculations distorts the normalized capex intensity of the business.
  • Use the EBITDA margin trend (Page 2) to identify the margin compression that began in FY2021 — this precedes the Ethiopia losses and reflects domestic competitive pricing pressure in voice and SMS revenue, which is structurally declining.
