Financial Analysis

01 · Business Problem

What does a decade of Safaricom's financials actually tell a CFO?

Safaricom is East Africa's most valuable listed company. Its annual reports are publicly available and widely read — but raw annual report data is not analysis. This project asks: what can structured SQL queries applied to 10 years of IFRS-adjusted audited financials reveal that a basic reading of the reports cannot?

The analytical challenge was building a database structure rigorous enough to support CFO-level queries — with proper accounting adjustments documented, anomalies flagged, and data lineage transparent — while keeping queries readable and reproducible.

What makes this project different: Most financial SQL projects use publicly scraped data with no accounting context. This project used Ernst & Young audited primary source financials, includes IFRS adjustment notes in the database itself via a dedicated data_notes table, and flags known anomalies (Ethiopia IAS 29, FY2022 capex) so analysts downstream don't misread the numbers.
02 · Data Source & Database Design

Audited primary source — IFRS-adjusted throughout

The data source is Safaricom's Ernst & Young audited annual financial statements spanning 10 fiscal years. All figures are in Kenya Shillings (KES millions) and have been adjusted to the same IFRS basis across all years to enable valid period comparisons.

data_notes table — accounting transparency built in

A dedicated data_notes table documents every IFRS adjustment, restatement, and accounting anomaly directly in the database. This means any analyst running queries downstream sees the same context the original analyst used — no hidden assumptions, no unexplained adjustments. This is a professional-grade design choice that most financial SQL projects skip entirely.

03 · Analytical Views

3 reusable views for CFO-level reporting

v_revenue_breakdown
Revenue split by line: Mobile Services, M-PESA, Fixed Line, Ethiopia Subsidiary. Includes YoY growth calculation and M-PESA share of total. Designed to be queried directly for any fiscal year range.
v_margin_analysis
EBITDA, EBIT, and Net Profit margin by year — all calculated from IFRS-adjusted line items. Excludes the Ethiopia IAS 29 hyperinflation gain from FY2023 adjusted figures. Documents the exclusion in the data_notes join.
v_capex_intensity
Capex as a percentage of revenue by year. Flags FY2022 as an outlier with a note from the data_notes table explaining the Ethiopia market entry cost vs. infrastructure capex distinction.

Sample view — revenue breakdown with M-PESA share:

-- v_revenue_breakdown: revenue by line with YoY growth and M-PESA share
CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW v_revenue_breakdown AS
SELECT
  f.fiscal_year,
  f.mobile_services_revenue,
  f.mpesa_revenue,
  f.fixed_line_revenue,
  f.ethiopia_revenue,
  f.total_revenue,
  -- M-PESA share of group revenue
  ROUND(
    f.mpesa_revenue * 100.0 / NULLIF(f.total_revenue, 0), 2
  ) AS mpesa_share_pct,
  -- YoY total revenue growth
  ROUND(
    (f.total_revenue - LAG(f.total_revenue) OVER (ORDER BY f.fiscal_year))
    * 100.0 /
    NULLIF(LAG(f.total_revenue) OVER (ORDER BY f.fiscal_year), 0), 2
  ) AS revenue_yoy_growth_pct
FROM financials f
ORDER BY f.fiscal_year;
04 · CFO-Level Queries

6 queries built to answer real strategic questions

01
10-year revenue trend with M-PESA contribution
Queries v_revenue_breakdown — shows how M-PESA grew from ~27% of revenue in FY2014 to ~42% in FY2023, structurally reshaping the business
02
EBITDA margin trend — reported vs. adjusted
Queries v_margin_analysis — shows both reported margin (including Ethiopia IAS 29 gain) and adjusted margin (excluding it) side by side for FY2023
03
Capex intensity — identifying the FY2022 strategic anomaly
Queries v_capex_intensity with a JOIN to data_notes — surfaces the FY2022 Ethiopia entry cost with the accounting note inline
04
M-PESA COVID zero-rating inflection point
LAG() window function — measures the FY2020 M-PESA revenue dip, FY2021 recovery, and compares the post-COVID baseline to the pre-COVID trend to quantify the structural uplift
05
Revenue per employee proxy — operational efficiency trend
DIVIDE pattern — total revenue divided by headcount data by year. Shows whether Safaricom is becoming more or less efficient per employee as it scales
06
Dividend payout ratio vs. free cash flow — sustainability check
Calculates dividend payments as a percentage of free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex) — flags years where payout exceeded FCF generation, which is a red flag for dividend sustainability
05 · Key Findings

What 10 years of SQL analysis reveals

M-PESA is now the business: The revenue breakdown query shows M-PESA grew from 27% of group revenue in FY2014 to 42% in FY2023 — and the trajectory continues. Safaricom is no longer a telecoms company with a payments division. It is a financial services platform that happens to own a telecoms network.
FY2023 EBITDA is overstated by the Ethiopia IAS 29 gain: The adjusted margin query shows that if you exclude the hyperinflation accounting gain, FY2023 EBITDA margin is approximately 3–4 percentage points lower than reported. This is a material difference for any investor or analyst using reported figures without adjustment.
FY2022 capex is not an infrastructure cycle — it is the Ethiopia bet: The capex intensity query, joined to the data_notes table, makes this explicit in the database itself. The Ethiopia market entry capitalized cost drove capex/revenue from ~18% (FY2021) to ~29% (FY2022). Normalizing for this, the underlying capex profile of the Kenyan business is stable and declining.
Dividend payout exceeded free cash flow in FY2022: The dividend sustainability query flagged FY2022 as a year where Safaricom paid out more in dividends than it generated in free cash flow — a signal that the Ethiopia investment was straining capital allocation decisions simultaneously.
06 · Recommendations

CFO-level actions from the SQL findings

  • Separate M-PESA reporting from telecoms reporting in internal planning models. The two businesses have different growth rates, cost structures, and capital requirements. Managing them as one unit obscures strategic allocation decisions.
  • Exclude the Ethiopia IAS 29 hyperinflation gain from any FY2023 performance-linked compensation or bonus calculations. The gain is an accounting artifact, not operating performance. Using reported figures rewards the accounting team, not the business team.
  • Model the Ethiopia operation separately in capex forecasting. Including Ethiopia capex in the group capex model will systematically overstate future capex requirements for the Kenyan business — which is the cash-generative core of the group.
  • Review the dividend policy in light of the FY2022 FCF coverage ratio. A payout ratio above 100% of FCF is sustainable for one year but signals that future dividend growth must be funded by debt or capex reduction — both of which have strategic implications.
